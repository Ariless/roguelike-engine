**9 of 9 passed.**

Not covered, stated rather than implied: the spectral (DFT) test, Maurer's
universal statistical test, linear complexity, and the template-matching family.
Those require sequence lengths in the millions of bits per run. For a 32-bit
generator driving a game simulation they are out of scope; claiming full
SP 800-22 coverage would be the more impressive and less true statement.

**Known limitation of the generator.** The seed is truncated to 32 bits
(`rng.ts`, `seed >>> 0`), so there are exactly 2³² distinguishable streams, and
mulberry32 advances by a fixed constant — meaning a seed selects an entry point
into one shared stream rather than an independent stream. Safe for sequential
seeds, unsafe if seeds are ever drawn from a clock or a counter with a stride.
Recorded as BUG-15 and pinned by tests.

## Open defects affecting these figures

| ID | Effect on the numbers above |
|---|---|
| BUG-14 | Two of four enemies do not perform actions their specification declares. The necromancer deals no direct damage at all, so every class shows an inflated win rate against it, and the class-level corridors above are skewed by that column. |
| BUG-16 | A UI test asserts a weaker condition than its name claims, so the mechanic missing in BUG-14 appears covered at the UI layer. |
| BUG-15 | Seed space is 2³² and streams are not independent by construction. Not a defect in the results above (seeds here are sequential), but it bounds how many genuinely independent runs are obtainable. |

These are listed because they change how the figures should be read. A pack that
showed only the green parts would be a brochure.
